    Editor’s letter

    This issue follows a career shaped by engineering, operations and, most recently, artificial intelligence. The common thread is simple: make complex work visible, then make it better.

    Ritika
    The Interview06

    The Interview

    An analytical, technically grounded project professional on structure, stakeholders and AI.

    Ritika Sonaavane pairs an engineer’s precision with a project manager’s eye for people. She has gathered requirements and built schedules as a consultant, solved operational problems at Amazon, coordinated repair projects for CPR by Assurant, and now leads a team designing an AI-enabled process for a live client while completing her MS in Project Management at Northeastern University.

    How would you describe what you do?
    I turn objectives into plans people can follow: requirements, schedules, budgets and risks, all visible in one place. At IT Works Infotech that structure contributed to a 15% faster timeline and 10% lower project costs.
    Where does AI fit into your work?
    On my capstone for Massey Services, our team used ChatGPT Enterprise and Microsoft Copilot Studio to build workflow logic directly from operational field data. I see AI as a way to give teams better visibility and faster decisions.
    Where does engineering come in?
    My Computer Engineering degree means I’m comfortable with SQL, Python and technical teams, and I can translate their work clearly for business stakeholders.
    What comes next?
    A project management or business analysis role. I’m open to relocation, wherever the right team is.
    “Make complex work visible, then make it better.”
